Who It's For

ACA coverage may be right for you if…

💼
You're Self-Employed
Freelancers, contractors, and business owners without employer coverage can find individual plans on the marketplace.
🏢
Your Job Doesn't Offer It
If your employer doesn't provide benefits or the plan isn't affordable, you likely qualify for marketplace coverage.
🔄
You Recently Lost Coverage
Losing job-based insurance, aging off a parent's plan, or losing Medicaid may trigger a Special Enrollment Period.
👨‍👩‍👧
You're Starting a Family
Marriage, having or adopting a child, and other family changes can open a Special Enrollment Period.
📍
You Recently Moved
Moving to a new area with new plan availability can qualify you for enrollment outside Open Enrollment.
❓
You're Not Sure
That's exactly what AllShield agents are for. A quick call tells you where you stand — free, no pressure.
Enrollment Windows

When you can enroll

// Open Enrollment Period

November 1 – January 15

The main window each year. Anyone can enroll or change their ACA plan — no qualifying event required. Coverage starting January 1 requires enrollment by December 15.

// Special Enrollment Period

Year-Round — with a qualifying event

Life events unlock enrollment any time. You typically have 60 days to act.

FAQ

Common Questions

Have a question that's not here? We'll answer it directly.

The Affordable Care Act created a health insurance marketplace and made financial assistance available to millions of Americans without employer coverage. It also prohibits insurers from denying coverage based on pre-existing conditions.
Premium tax credits are financial subsidies that reduce your monthly health insurance premium. Eligibility is based on household size and income. Many people qualify for significant assistance — sometimes to $0 per month.
Your deductible is what you pay before insurance covers most services. A copay is a fixed amount for a specific service. Your out-of-pocket maximum is the most you'll pay in a year — after that, insurance covers 100% of covered services.
HMO plans typically have lower premiums but require you to use a specific network and get referrals for specialists. PPO plans offer more flexibility — you can see most doctors without a referral — but usually cost more per month.
Yes — through a Special Enrollment Period triggered by qualifying life events: losing coverage, getting married, having a baby, moving, and others. You typically have 60 days from the event.
